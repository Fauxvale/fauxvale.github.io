Part One - Stability

Viva New Vegas is installed and running - nice work. Now Fauxvale Edition begins (again). Part One is all about stability: a few quick INI tweaks and one new mod layered on top of your VNV setup. Everything here happens inside Mod Organizer 2 (MO2), the same tool VNV had you set up.

  1. Create your Fauxvale Edition separator

    Open MO2. In the left-hand mod list, scroll to the bottom of your VNV setup and find the Extended Finish separator. Right-click empty space in the list and choose Create separator. Name it exactly Fauxvale Edition.

    Drag the new separator so it sits directly below Extended Finish. Everything we install from here on lives under this separator - that keeps Fauxvale Edition cleanly stacked on top of VNV.

  2. Force fullscreen in FalloutCustom.ini

    At the top of MO2, click the Tools button and choose INI Editor. In the editor, select the FalloutCustom.ini tab - VNV already populated it with the correct base settings.

    Scroll to the [Display] section and make this change, then hit Save:

    [Display] bFull Screen=0 ▸ bFull Screen=1

  3. Enable the flip model in NVTF.ini

    Open this file (it lives inside your MO2 mods folder):

    ...\mods\NVTF - INI\NVSE\Plugins\NVTF.ini

    Find bUseFlipModel and change its value to 1, then save:

    bUseFlipModel=0 ▸ bUseFlipModel=1

  4. Install OneTweak but Really Updated

    Open the mod's Nexus page, switch to the Files tab, and click Mod Manager Download on the main file. Nexus hands the download straight to MO2 - you'll see it arrive in MO2's Downloads pane.

    OneTweak but Really Updated

    Borderless Windowed Mode for Fallout New Vegas.

    Open on Nexus ↗

    In the Downloads pane, double-click the finished download to install it (keep the default name). Once it appears in your mod list, drag it under your Fauxvale Edition separator and tick its checkbox to activate it.

Want the details? For a deeper explanation of what these INI settings do and what OneTweak but Really Updated changes under the hood, see the Fallout: New Vegas performance guide - entirely optional, just there if you're interested.
